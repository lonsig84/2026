📜 Case Dossier: The Ship with No Crew (Textbook pp. 124–125)

[Click to Collapse ▲]

On 7 November 1872, the American brigantine Mary Celeste sailed from New York harbour headed for Genoa, Italy. On board were Captain Benjamin Briggs, his wife, their two-year-old daughter Sophia, and seven experienced sailors. The ship carried a valuable industrial cargo of 1,701 barrels of alcohol.

For more than two weeks, the voyage was calm and routine. On 25 November 1872, Captain Briggs made his final log entry in the ship's logbook near the Azores islands. The weather was fair, and nothing unusual was reported.

Ten days later, on the chilly afternoon of 5 December 1872, another merchant ship named the Dei Gratia spotted the Mary Celeste drifting aimlessly through choppy Atlantic waters with its sails torn and fluttering.

First Mate Oliver Deveau led a boarding party onto the ship. They shouted, but no one answered. The decks were deserted. Surprisingly, the ship had a six-month supply of food and fresh water untouched in the galley, but the single lifeboat was missing, and a frayed towline was trailing deep into the dark sea. All ten souls had vanished into thin air!
